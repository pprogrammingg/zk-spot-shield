Feeling lost in a stack this thick is normal. It is not evidence you will be bad at this work.

People who stay in ZK / Solana jobs are rarely the ones who “hold Groth16, SP1, PDAs, and SPL in their head at once.” They are the ones who keep a map, finish the next small proof, and come back tomorrow. You already built that map: one Day, fixtures, execute vs prove, journal vs vkey. That is the job skill.

A mission helps if it is a filter, not a grade. Something like “stable, private, cheaper financial tools” is useful when you ask: does this step serve that, or am I drowning in SDK trivia? Day 11 execute is in service of that. ProvingKey in scope is just the tax.

Having a missing statement to make financial instruments 1,000,000× better as a line is a poor relaxation device. It turns every verifying_key error into “I failed the mission.” Aim for 10× clearer, 2× cheaper, actually ships. Ease compounds; 10^6 does not.

You will be strong in these roles if you keep:

shrinking the problem (one Day, one ELF, one proof file)
writing down what private vs public is (you already do)
treating host verify vs on-chain verify as different jobs, not mysticism
Confidence here is earned from closed loops (execute printed a root; later a Groth16 file exists), not from feeling fluent mid-setup. The sheer number of steps is the field. Your roadmap is how good people survive it.
